# ReqMind AI — Scientific Limitations, Risks & Boundary Conditions

**Version:** 2.0 (Phase 8 Final Release)  
**Module:** Traitement Automatique du Langage Naturel (TALN)  
**Scope:** Academic & Production Limitations Analysis  

---

## 1. Introduction

An essential component of rigorous Natural Language Processing (TALN) research is identifying the operational boundaries, inductive biases, and limitations of the proposed architecture. This document delineates the known constraints of ReqMind AI across its data representations, classification algorithms, quality heuristics, and runtime environment.

---

## 2. Ingestion & Segmentation Boundaries

### 2.1. Structural Complexity in Industrial Specifications
ReqMind AI employs a hierarchical, regex-assisted rule engine to segment requirements while preserving character-exact document offsets. While highly effective on formatted lists, numbered items, and bulleted text, it exhibits known edge-case limitations:
- **Complex Embedded Tables:** Requirements formatted inside ASCII tables or unstructured multi-column layouts can lead to fragmented sentences.
- **Compound Multi-Sentence Requirements:** Long paragraphs that bundle multiple functional capabilities into a single narrative are treated as a single requirement unit unless segmented by explicit bullet delimiters or standard punctuation.
- **Language Scope:** The segmentation and normalization modules assume modern English orthography and punctuation.

---

## 3. Classification Subsystem Limitations

### 3.1. Binary Abstraction vs Multi-Concern Requirements
ReqMind AI frames requirement categorization as a binary classification problem ($\mathcal{Y} \in \{\text{FR}, \text{NFR}\}$):
- **Oversimplification of Hybrid Requirements:** Industrial requirements frequently exhibit multi-concern characteristics. For instance:
  > *"The system shall authenticate users via OAuth 2.0 within 300 ms."*  
  This sentence specifies both a functional capability (*user authentication*) and a non-functional SLA (*latency constraint*). Binary classification forces a forced-choice single label, masking secondary concerns.
- **Absence of Hierarchical Sub-Categories:** Non-Functional Requirements span diverse ISO/IEC 25010 sub-disciplines (Security, Performance, Usability, Reliability, Maintainability). The current classifier does not predict specific NFR sub-types.

### 3.2. Lexical Bias in Bag-of-Words Models
As demonstrated during Phase 8 benchmarking:
- **Template Memorization:** The TF-IDF + SVM models achieved > 99% accuracy on the partitioned validation set by exploiting repeated boilerplate phrases, but dropped to 76.7% – 80.0% on the 60-example human-annotated benchmark.
- **Vocabulary Drift:** Classical models fail when confronted with domain-specific jargon or syntactic synonyms absent from the initial training corpus.

### 3.3. Transformer Resource Requirements
While **Fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-small** demonstrates superior generalization (96.67% accuracy on challenging edge cases), it introduces practical trade-offs:
- **Inference Latency:** DeBERTa requires **~14.66 – 23.37 ms** per requirement on CPU, compared to **~0.28 – 0.46 ms** for the SVM models (~50× slower).
- **Memory & Storage:** Model weights require **~560 MB** of disk space and ~650 MB of RAM, making it less suitable for ultra-low-power embedded edge devices without quantization (e.g., INT8/ONNX).

## 4. Ambiguity & Quality Detection Boundaries

The ambiguity engine (`src/reqmind/ambiguity.py`) implements deterministic pattern matching based on IEEE 830 guidelines:
- **Context-Insensitive Lexicon Matching:** The engine flags tokens from curated dictionaries of vague adverbs, subjective terms, and non-binding modals. Consequently:
  - *Domain-Specific False Positives:* Technical terms containing flagged words (e.g., *"fast Fourier transform"*, *"high-speed bus"*, *"graceful degradation"*) can trigger false quality alerts.
  - *Acceptable Idiomatic Phrases:* Phrases like *"the system shall appropriately handle exceptions"* are flagged, even when the exact exception protocol is detailed in an adjacent paragraph.
- **Syntactic Ambiguity Blind Spots:** Pure regex matching cannot detect deep syntactic ambiguities such as:
  - *Prepositional Phrase Attachment:* *"The system shall display notifications to users with urgent priority."* (Does "urgent priority" qualify the users or the notifications?)
  - *Scope Ambiguity:* *"The system shall print invoices and receipts if requested."* (Does "if requested" apply to both or only receipts?)
- **Mitigation:** The system introduces **Human-in-the-Loop Span Dismissal**, enabling engineers to dismiss false alarms without altering the raw requirement text.

## 5. Semantic Similarity & Duplicate Detection Boundaries

The semantic similarity engine (`src/reqmind/similarity.py`) relies on dense sentence representations generated by `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2`:
- **Cosine Distance vs Formal Logical Equivalence:** High cosine similarity indicates that two sentences occupy nearby points in semantic embedding space; it does **not** prove mathematical equivalence.
- **Numeric & Negation Sensitivity:** Bi-encoders can struggle to differentiate sentences with opposite polarities or different numeric values when the surrounding context is identical.
  - *Example:* *"The system shall timeout after 10 seconds"* vs *"The system shall timeout after 60 seconds"*.
  - *Mitigation:* ReqMind AI enforces dedicated **Negation Guards** and **SLA Numeric Conflict Guards** to flag these divergences explicitly.
- **Non-Destructive Guardrail:** The system strictly prohibits automated requirement merging or deletion; all candidate duplicates require human confirmation.

---

## 6. Dataset Annotation & Human Subjectivity Challenges

- **Inter-Annotator Disagreement:** Software engineering literature consistently reports significant variance among human annotators when distinguishing FR from NFR (Cohen's $\kappa$ typically ranges from 0.65 to 0.80). What a software architect classifies as a functional security feature (e.g., logging or access control) may be labeled as an operational non-functional constraint by a systems auditor.
- **Evaluation Discipline:** Benchmarks must be treated as indicative guidance rather than absolute truth. The 60-example test set reflects strict boundary interpretations, but alternative domain contexts may warrant adjusted thresholds.

---

## 7. Scope Boundary Regarding Phase 7 (Contradiction Detection)

- **Status:** Phase 7 (Natural Language Inference contradiction detection) was intentionally excluded from the production release and remains an experimental research module (`src/reqmind/contradiction.py`).
- **Rationale:** Cross-requirement contradiction detection using general NLI models (e.g., MNLI) suffers from unacceptable false-positive rates on complex engineering specifications without fine-tuning on formal requirement ontologies. Rather than deploying an unreliable heuristic, ReqMind AI focuses its computational guarantees on verified classification, ambiguity detection, and similarity ranking.
